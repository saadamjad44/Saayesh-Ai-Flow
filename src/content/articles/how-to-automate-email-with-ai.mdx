Answering the same kinds of emails every day is one of the easiest places for AI to help a small business. Pricing questions, booking requests, and "did you receive my order?" messages follow patterns that AI can draft replies for. This tutorial covers three practical methods: **Gmail with Zapier**, **Outlook with Power Automate**, and **dedicated AI email tools**. It also covers the safety checks that stop automated replies from embarrassing you.

This is a research-based tutorial built from each vendor's official documentation and pricing pages, checked on 15 September 2026. We have not tested these setups hands-on for this article, and we do not publish time-saved figures because they depend on your email volume.

## Before you build: write a reply playbook

Every method works better when the AI has clear, approved information to work from. Write a short document with:

1. **Your common email types.** For example: pricing questions, availability or booking requests, order status, and general enquiries.
2. **Approved answers for each type.** Include current prices, policies, links, and next steps you are happy to put in writing.
3. **Your tone.** A few sentences describing how you write, plus one or two real replies you are proud of.
4. **Escalation rules.** Topics the AI must never answer, such as complaints, refunds, legal questions, or anything involving a discount.
5. **A fallback instruction.** What the AI should do when the answer is not in your playbook, such as writing "[NEEDS YOUR INPUT]" in the draft.

Keep this playbook up to date. An automation that drafts from last year's prices creates more work, not less.

## Method 1: Gmail and Zapier

Zapier connects Gmail to an AI step and back again. Zapier lists Gmail triggers such as **New Labeled Email** and **New Email Matching Search**, and actions including **Create Draft Reply**, **Add Label to Email**, and **Reply to Email**. Multi-step Zaps and AI by Zapier are listed on the Professional plan and above.

### Step 1: Label the emails you want to automate

Create a Gmail label such as "AI draft". Apply it with a Gmail filter (for example, emails sent to your contact address or containing "pricing" in the subject), or apply it by hand at first so you control exactly which emails enter the automation.

### Step 2: Create the Zap and set the trigger

In Zapier, create a new Zap. Choose **Gmail** as the trigger app and **New Labeled Email** as the event, then select your "AI draft" label. Test the trigger with a recent real email.

### Step 3: Add an AI step

Add an **AI by Zapier** step. Map the email's sender name, subject, and body into it, and paste your playbook into the instructions. Example instructions:

```text
You draft email replies for [business name].
Use ONLY the approved answers below. Never invent prices, dates, or policies.
If the answer is not in the approved answers, write [NEEDS YOUR INPUT] where it belongs.
If the email is a complaint, refund request, or legal question, output only: ESCALATE
Write in a friendly, concise tone. Sign off as [your name].

Approved answers:
[paste your playbook here]
```

### Step 4: Stop emails that should not get a draft

Add a **Filter** step that only continues if the AI output does not contain "ESCALATE". You can add a second path that notifies you about escalated emails instead.

### Step 5: Create a draft reply

Add a **Gmail** action with the **Create Draft Reply** event. Map the original email's thread and the AI output as the reply body. The reply now waits in your drafts, in the same thread, for you to review.

### Step 6: Test, then turn it on

Test with several real past emails, including an unusual one and one that should be escalated. Turn the Zap on, then check its run history and your drafts daily for the first weeks.

For more Gmail-based workflows, including email triage, see examples 1 and 3 in our [Zapier AI automation examples](/ai-automation/zapier-ai-automation-examples/).

## Method 2: Outlook and Power Automate

Power Automate is Microsoft's automation platform. According to Microsoft's documentation, the Office 365 Outlook connector is a standard connector, and the Power Automate Free license lets work or school accounts create cloud flows with standard connectors. The AI step uses the AI Builder **Run a prompt** action, which needs AI Builder capacity. Microsoft lists 5,000 AI Builder credits per month with each Power Automate Premium license, and notes that prompts are available only in some regions.

### Step 1: Create a prompt

In Power Automate, create a custom prompt with one input, such as "EmailBody". In the prompt instructions, include your playbook, the rule to use only approved answers, and the escalation and fallback rules from the Gmail example above.

### Step 2: Create an automated cloud flow

Create an **automated cloud flow** with the Office 365 Outlook trigger **When a new email arrives (V3)**. Use the trigger's options to limit it to a specific folder, sender, or subject, so the flow only runs on the emails you intend.

### Step 3: Add the Run a prompt action

Add the **Run a prompt** action, choose your prompt, and map the email body from the trigger into its input.

### Step 4: Add a human review

Microsoft's documentation describes adding human review after a prompt with the Approvals action **Start and wait for an approval of text**. Put the prompt's output in the suggested text and assign the approval to yourself. You can then review and edit the text before continuing.

### Step 5: Send only approved replies

Add a **Condition** that checks whether the approval outcome equals "Approve". In the yes branch, add the Office 365 Outlook action **Reply to email (V3)** using the approved text. In the no branch, do nothing, or notify yourself to reply by hand.

### Step 6: Test and monitor

Test the flow with real emails and check the flow's run history regularly. If your organization has an IT administrator, confirm that Power Automate and AI Builder are allowed before building.

## Method 3: Dedicated AI email tools

If you would rather not build automations, AI email apps and built-in assistants put AI drafting inside your inbox. Examples from vendors' official pages:

- **Superhuman:** its plans page lists AI email features including Auto Drafts, Write with AI with voice and tone matching, Instant Reply, and Auto Labels on the Business plan, at $33 per member per month billed annually or $40 monthly.
- **Shortwave:** its pricing page lists AI-powered filters, AI autocomplete, and summaries, with the Business plan at $30 per seat per month and a 14-day free trial.
- **Gemini in Gmail:** Google lists the Gemini AI assistant in Gmail on Google Workspace Business Starter, at $7 per user per month.
- **Copilot in Outlook:** Microsoft's support pages describe drafting messages from a prompt and adjusting length and tone. It is part of Microsoft 365 Copilot, which requires an eligible Microsoft 365 Business plan.

How to set up a dedicated tool well:

1. **Confirm it supports your email provider** and read how it stores and uses your email data.
2. **Start with a trial** on your real inbox.
3. **Add your playbook** wherever the tool accepts instructions or saved snippets.
4. **Set up AI sorting first,** such as labels or filters, before relying on AI replies.
5. **Keep drafts as drafts** until you trust the results on your own emails.

## When fully automatic replies make sense

Some emails are safe to answer without review, as long as the reply cannot promise anything:

- Confirming that a message or form was received, with your usual response time.
- Out-of-hours replies that point to your FAQ or booking page.
- Sending a fixed resource someone requested, such as a price list you publish anyway.

Keep a human review for pricing negotiations, complaints, refunds, legal, medical, or financial topics, and your most important customers.
