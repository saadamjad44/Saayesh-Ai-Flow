AI pricing looks simple until you try to budget for it. A tool might be free for one person, $20 per month for more usage, and priced per seat once your team joins. This guide breaks down what AI actually costs a small business at three budget levels, names real tools at each level with their current list prices, and shows the costs that do not appear on pricing pages.

### Free: what $0 gets you

Free plans let you find out whether AI helps with a specific task before you spend anything. The limits are what separate them from paid plans:

- **Claude Free:** a general AI assistant at no recurring charge. Anthropic's pricing page notes that usage limits apply.
- **Grammarly Free:** writing suggestions with 100 AI prompts per month.
- **Otter.ai Basic:** 300 transcription minutes per month, with a 30-minute limit per meeting.
- **Zapier Free:** 100 tasks per month and two-step Zaps only. AI by Zapier is listed on paid plans, not the free plan.
- **Microsoft Copilot Chat:** listed at no additional cost for users with eligible Microsoft 365 plans, so check your existing subscription.

A free setup suits a solo owner who uses AI occasionally. It stops being enough when you hit usage caps during a busy week or need automations with more than two steps.

### $20 to $50 per month: one person, paid tools

This is where most individual paid plans sit. Typical options at list price:

- **Claude Pro:** $20 per month billed monthly, or $17 per month with annual billing ($200 billed up front).
- **Grammarly Pro:** $12 per month, with 2,000 AI prompts per member per month.
- **Otter.ai Pro:** $16.99 per user per month billed monthly, with 1,200 transcription minutes per user and up to 90 minutes per meeting. Otter lists lower prices for annual billing.
- **Zapier Professional:** from $19.99 per month billed annually, or $29.99 billed monthly, for 750 tasks per month, with multi-step Zaps and AI by Zapier.
- **Google Workspace Business Starter:** $7 per user per month, or $6.30 for the first 12 months for new customers with up to 20 users. Google lists the Gemini AI assistant in Gmail on this plan, alongside business email and storage.

Combining two of these, such as an AI assistant plus an automation platform, usually lands between $20 and $50 per month for one person.

### $100 or more per month: teams and heavier use

Costs pass $100 per month in three common ways:

1. **Higher usage tiers.** Claude Max starts at $100 per month for one person who needs more usage than Pro allows.
2. **More seats.** Claude Team standard seats are $20 per seat per month billed annually, or $25 billed monthly, with a two-seat minimum. Premium seats are $100 per seat billed annually, or $125 monthly.
3. **Business platforms and add-ons.** Zapier Team starts at $69 per month billed annually, or $103.50 monthly, for 2,000 tasks and shared workspaces. Microsoft 365 Copilot Business is $18 per user per month on an annual commitment, a promotional price Microsoft lists through 31 December 2026 (the standard annual price shown is $21), or $25.20 billed monthly. It requires an eligible Microsoft 365 Business plan.

Dedicated AI tools for specific jobs add up quickly too. For example, Superhuman lists its Business plan, which includes its AI email features, at $33 per member per month billed annually or $40 monthly.

## Costs that do not appear on the pricing page

The subscription is only part of what AI costs. Budget for these as well:

- **Per-seat pricing.** Team plans multiply with every person, and some have seat minimums.
- **Usage limits.** Tasks on Zapier, transcription minutes on Otter.ai, AI prompts on Grammarly, and usage limits on AI assistants can force an upgrade in a busy month.
- **Introductory prices.** Google's first-year Workspace discount and Microsoft's Copilot Business promotional price both end, so budget for the standard price.
- **Annual commitments.** Cheaper per month, but you pay up front and cannot easily cancel mid-term.
- **Tax.** Anthropic's pricing page, for example, states that prices shown do not include applicable tax.
- **Your time.** Setting up tools, writing instructions, and reviewing AI output all take hours that have a real cost to your business.
- **Overlap.** Paying for two general AI assistants rarely doubles the value. Check what your email suite, workspace, or accounting software already includes.

## How to set your AI budget

1. **List your recurring tasks.** Note the tasks you do every week that are repetitive, text-heavy, or involve copying information between apps.
2. **Check what you already pay for.** Your email suite, workspace, or accounting software may already include AI features.
3. **Start free.** Use free plans on one real task for two weeks.
4. **Pay monthly first.** Upgrade only the tool that proved useful, on monthly billing, so you can cancel easily.
5. **Estimate the value.** Compare the monthly cost with the time the tool saves you, minus the time you spend checking its work. Use your own records, not a vendor's claims.
6. **Move to annual billing only after a few months** of regular use.
7. **Review every quarter.** Cancel tools you have not used in the past month.

A simple way to total your budget: for each tool, multiply the seat price by the number of people who need it, add any usage add-ons, then add the tools together. If the total feels high, cut the tool with the weakest link to a real task first.
