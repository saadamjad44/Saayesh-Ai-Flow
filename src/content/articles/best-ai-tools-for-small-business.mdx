Small business owners do not need fifty AI tools. They need a few that take real work off their plate. This list picks nine tools across the jobs that fill a small business owner's week: writing, email, documents, meetings, scheduling, bookkeeping, customer support, and connecting it all together. For each one we explain what it helps with, what it costs, and where it falls short.

## How we picked these tools

This is a research-based list. We have not tested these tools hands-on for this article. To choose them, we looked for tools that:

- **Cover a common small business job** rather than a niche need.
- **Have documented AI features** on the vendor's own site.
- **Offer a free plan, trial, or small-business pricing,** so owners can start without an enterprise contract.
- **Publish pricing** or at least a clear plan structure.

We deliberately picked one tool per job. Most categories have good alternatives, and we mention some of them. The right choice often depends on software you already use.

## Writing and communication

### 1. ChatGPT: everyday writing, planning, and research

**What it helps with:** drafting emails and proposals, turning notes into documents, brainstorming marketing ideas, summarizing long material, and working through problems in plain language. For a business owner who covers every role, one flexible assistant is often the most valuable AI tool.

**Pricing:** free plan; ChatGPT Go at $8 per month in the US; ChatGPT Plus at $20 per month. ChatGPT Business costs $25 per user per month billed monthly, or $20 billed annually, with a two-seat minimum.

**Limitations:** it can be wrong with confidence, so check facts and numbers. Business plans need at least two seats. Plan features and usage limits vary, so compare tiers before upgrading.

**Best for:** owners who want one assistant for many kinds of work. Claude is a strong alternative. Our [best AI writing tools roundup](/ai-writing-tools/best-ai-writing-tools/) compares both.

### 2. Grammarly: polished customer-facing writing

**What it helps with:** catching mistakes and improving clarity and tone in emails, proposals, and website text as you write. According to Grammarly's plans page, Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustments, plagiarism and AI-text detection, a style guide and brand tone, and 2,000 AI prompts per member per month.

**Pricing:** free plan with 100 AI prompts per month; Pro listed at $12 per month; custom Enterprise pricing.

**Limitations:** it is built for editing and rewriting more than for producing long drafts from scratch.

**Best for:** anyone whose emails and proposals represent the business and who wants a consistent, professional tone.

## Email, documents, and knowledge

### 3. Google Workspace with Gemini: AI in your email and documents

**What it helps with:** drafting and summarizing inside Gmail and, on higher plans, Docs and Meet, without adding a separate app. Google's pricing page lists the Gemini AI assistant in Gmail and access to the Gemini app on Business Starter, with Gemini extending to Docs, Meet, and more on Business Standard.

**Pricing:** on Google's US pricing page, Business Starter was $7 per user per month, Business Standard $14, and Business Plus $22, with a 10% first-year discount on Starter and Standard for up to 20 users.

**Limitations:** Gemini in Docs requires Business Standard or above. It is most useful if Google Workspace is already your email and document platform. Microsoft 365 users should look at the AI options in their own suite instead.

**Best for:** small businesses already running on Gmail and Google Docs.

### 4. Notion: notes, documents, and team knowledge with AI

**What it helps with:** keeping procedures, client notes, and project documents in one workspace, then using Notion AI to draft, summarize, translate, and find answers across it. Notion's pricing page lists Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes, and Enterprise Search (beta) on the Business plan.

**Pricing:** free plan; Plus at $10 per member per month; Business at $20 per member per month, with up to 20% savings on annual billing. Free and Plus include only a limited trial of the AI features.

**Limitations:** full AI features start at Business. Notion takes time to set up well, and it pays off only if you actually keep your information there.

**Best for:** owners and small teams who want a single place for processes and knowledge.

## Meetings and time

### 5. Otter.ai: meeting transcripts and summaries

**What it helps with:** recording and transcribing meetings, then producing summaries so you can focus on the conversation rather than notes. Otter's plans also include a monthly allowance of AI chat queries for asking questions about your meetings.

**Pricing:** the free Basic plan includes 300 monthly transcription minutes with a 30-minute limit per meeting. Pro is $16.99 per user per month billed monthly, with 1,200 monthly minutes and a 90-minute limit per meeting. Business is $30 per user per month billed monthly, with a 4-hour meeting limit. Annual billing is cheaper; check [Otter's pricing page](https://otter.ai/pricing) for current annual rates.

**Limitations:** the free plan's 30-minute meeting cap is short for client calls. Always tell participants when you record, and check the recording consent rules that apply where you and they are located.

**Best for:** consultants and service businesses with frequent client or sales calls.

### 6. Reclaim.ai: scheduling and protecting focus time

**What it helps with:** managing your calendar automatically, including scheduling links for bookings, blocking focus time, planning recurring habits, and adding buffer time around meetings. Reclaim's pricing page lists Focus Time, Habits, Buffer Time, and Smart Meetings on its paid plans.

**Pricing:** the free Lite plan includes 1 scheduling link, 1 calendar sync, and a 1-week scheduling range. Paid Starter and Business plans are priced per seat with higher limits. The page showed introductory offers when we checked, so see [Reclaim's pricing page](https://reclaim.ai/pricing) for the rate that applies to you.

**Limitations:** the free plan's 1-week scheduling range and single link are restrictive for a busy practice. It works best when your whole schedule lives in a connected calendar.

**Best for:** owners whose week is fragmented by meetings and who struggle to protect time for focused work.

## Money

### 7. QuickBooks Online: bookkeeping with AI assistance

**What it helps with:** Intuit describes AI agents in QuickBooks Online that can categorize transactions, detect anomalies, help reconcile accounts, and send AI-generated reminders for overdue invoices. For a small business, that can reduce the admin of keeping books current.

**Pricing:** QuickBooks Online plan prices and promotions change often and vary by country, so we have not listed a figure. Check the [QuickBooks pricing page](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/pricing/) and confirm which AI features are included on your plan and in your region.

**Limitations:** AI categorization still needs review, and mistakes in your books have real consequences at tax time. Keep your accountant or bookkeeper involved. Switching accounting software mid-year is also disruptive, so this is best considered when setting up or reviewing your system.

**Best for:** small businesses that want to keep bookkeeping current without doing every entry by hand.

## Customers

### 8. Tidio: customer support chat with an AI agent

**What it helps with:** live chat and ticketing for your website, with Lyro, Tidio's AI agent, answering common customer questions. According to Tidio's pricing page, the Starter plan includes live chat, ticketing, basic analytics, and 50 one-off Lyro conversations. Lyro is also sold as an add-on for ongoing AI conversations.

**Pricing:** Starter was listed at $290.04 per year billed annually, with 100 billable conversations and 10 seats. Growth starts at $590.04 per year billed annually. Lyro add-on pricing depends on conversation volume, so check [Tidio's pricing page](https://www.tidio.com/pricing/) for current rates.

**Limitations:** an AI agent is only as good as the help content you give it, and conversation-based pricing grows with volume. Keep a clear route to a human for complaints and complicated issues.

**Best for:** online stores and service businesses that answer the same customer questions again and again.

## Connecting everything

### 9. Zapier: connecting apps and adding AI steps

**What it helps with:** connecting the tools above so information flows between them automatically. For example, you can add new leads to a spreadsheet, send meeting notes to your task list, or use an AI step to summarize or categorize incoming messages. Zapier's pricing page lists AI by Zapier and AI fields on the Professional plan, and Copilot for building automations.

**Pricing:** free plan with 100 tasks per month and two-step Zaps only. Professional starts at $19.99 per month billed annually, or $29.99 billed monthly, for 750 tasks. Team starts at $69 per month billed annually for 2,000 tasks.

**Limitations:** multi-step automations need a paid plan, and busy automations can use up task allowances. Automations can fail quietly, so check them regularly.

**Best for:** owners who spend time copying information between apps. See our [Zapier AI automation examples](/ai-automation/zapier-ai-automation-examples/) for practical workflows.

## How to build your small business AI stack

You do not need all nine tools. A sensible order:

1. **Start with one general assistant** for writing and planning, such as ChatGPT.
2. **Check your existing software.** Your email suite, workspace, or accounting software may already include AI features you are not using.
3. **Add one specialist tool for your biggest time drain,** whether that is meetings, scheduling, or customer questions.
4. **Connect tools only when copy-and-paste becomes the bottleneck,** using an automation platform like Zapier.
5. **Review every quarter.** Cancel anything you have not used in the past month.
