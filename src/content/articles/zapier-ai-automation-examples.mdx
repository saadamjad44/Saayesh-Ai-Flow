Zapier connects thousands of apps so that an event in one, like a new email or form entry, can trigger actions in others. Adding AI steps means those workflows can handle messy, unstructured text too: sorting messages, extracting details, and drafting responses. Below are eight practical examples for small businesses, each broken down into the same five parts so you can adapt them to your own tools.

These examples are research-based workflow designs using features documented by Zapier, not reports of tested automations. App names and steps are suggestions; use the equivalent apps you already have.

## Zapier's AI features in brief

Before the examples, here is what Zapier's pricing page listed when we checked on 14 September 2026:

- **Zaps:** automated workflows made of a trigger and one or more actions.
- **AI by Zapier and AI fields:** built-in AI steps that work with data from earlier steps, listed on the Professional plan.
- **Copilot:** AI help for building automations. The free plan has a daily limit; Professional lists unlimited messages.
- **Filters, Paths, and Formatter:** tools to stop, branch, or clean up data in a Zap, listed on Professional.
- **Tables and Forms:** included on the free plan for storing data and collecting submissions.
- **Agents and Chatbots:** separate products with their own free and paid tiers.

Every example below uses more than two steps, so plan on a paid plan. Check [Zapier's pricing page](https://zapier.com/pricing) for current details and how tasks are counted.

## 1. Triage new customer emails

**Trigger:** a new email arrives in your support or contact inbox in Gmail.

**Tools involved:** Gmail, AI by Zapier, Paths by Zapier, Slack (or email notifications).

**Workflow:**

1. The Zap triggers on each new email in the chosen inbox or label.
2. An AI by Zapier step reads the subject and body and returns a category (for example: sales enquiry, support question, invoice, or other), an urgency level, and a one-sentence summary.
3. Paths by Zapier sends urgent messages down one path and everything else down another.

**Action:** urgent emails send you a Slack message with the summary. All emails get a Gmail label that matches their category.

**Expected benefit:** you see what needs attention first without reading every message, and your inbox stays organized by topic.

**Watch out for:** misclassified emails. Keep an "other" category and check it regularly, especially in the first few weeks.

## 2. Log and summarize website enquiries

**Trigger:** a new submission to your contact form in Zapier Forms, Typeform, or a similar form tool.

**Tools involved:** your form tool, AI by Zapier, Google Sheets or Zapier Tables, Gmail.

**Workflow:**

1. The Zap triggers when someone submits the form.
2. An AI by Zapier step turns the free-text message into structured fields: the service they want, their stated budget or timeline if mentioned, and a short summary.
3. A Filter step stops the Zap if the message looks like spam or is missing an email address.

**Action:** add a row to your leads sheet or table with the form data and AI fields, then email yourself the summary.

**Expected benefit:** every lead is logged consistently, and you can scan requests quickly before deciding who to call first.

**Watch out for:** invented details. Instruct the AI step to write "not stated" when information is missing rather than guessing.

## 3. Draft replies to common questions

**Trigger:** a new email in Gmail with a label you apply to routine questions, such as pricing or availability.

**Tools involved:** Gmail, AI by Zapier, Zapier Tables (optional, for your approved answers).

**Workflow:**

1. You, or the triage Zap in example 1, apply a "common question" label.
2. The Zap looks up your approved answers, stored in a table or in the AI step's instructions.
3. An AI by Zapier step drafts a reply using only those approved answers and the customer's message.

**Action:** create a draft reply in Gmail. Nothing is sent automatically.

**Expected benefit:** replies to repeat questions start from a consistent, accurate draft, so you spend your time on editing and personal touches.

**Watch out for:** answers that go beyond your approved information. Tell the AI step to say it does not know rather than invent policies or prices.

## 4. Turn meeting notes into tasks

**Trigger:** a new document in a Google Drive folder where your meeting notes or transcripts are saved.

**Tools involved:** Google Drive or Google Docs, AI by Zapier, Looping by Zapier, a task manager such as Asana, Todoist, or Trello.

**Workflow:**

1. The Zap triggers when a new notes document appears in the folder.
2. An AI by Zapier step extracts action items, with an owner and a due date where they are mentioned.
3. Looping by Zapier runs the next step once for each action item.

**Action:** create a task for each action item in your task manager, linked back to the original notes.

**Expected benefit:** follow-ups from calls do not get lost in a document you never reopen.

**Watch out for:** vague or duplicate tasks. Ask the AI step to skip items without a clear action, and review new tasks after each meeting.

## 5. Repurpose new blog posts for social media

**Trigger:** a new post in your blog's RSS feed.

**Tools involved:** RSS by Zapier, AI by Zapier, Google Sheets or Google Docs, Slack or Gmail.

**Workflow:**

1. RSS by Zapier triggers when a new post is published.
2. An AI by Zapier step writes draft social posts for the platforms you use, based on the post's title and summary, following your tone instructions.
3. The drafts are saved with the post link.

**Action:** add the drafts to a content sheet or document and notify you that they are ready to review.

**Expected benefit:** each new article comes with a starting point for promotion, so publishing and sharing happen together.

**Watch out for:** posting AI drafts unchecked. Keep a human review step before anything is published, because platform tone and accuracy matter.

## 6. Record expenses from emailed receipts

**Trigger:** a new email in Gmail with a label such as "receipts", applied by a Gmail filter or manually.

**Tools involved:** Gmail, AI by Zapier, Filter by Zapier, Google Sheets.

**Workflow:**

1. The Zap triggers on new emails with the receipts label.
2. An AI by Zapier step extracts the vendor, date, total amount, and currency from the email text.
3. A Filter step continues only if the amount and date were found.

**Action:** add a row to your expenses sheet. Emails where extraction failed get a separate label so you can handle them by hand.

**Expected benefit:** a running expenses log that is ready for your bookkeeping review, instead of receipts scattered across your inbox.

**Watch out for:** receipts where the details are only in an attachment, and any extraction mistakes. Check totals against the original emails before they reach your accounts, and keep your bookkeeper in the loop.

## 7. Tag customer feedback by theme

**Trigger:** a new response to your feedback or review form.

**Tools involved:** your form tool, AI by Zapier, Zapier Tables, Digest by Zapier, Gmail.

**Workflow:**

1. The Zap triggers on each new feedback response.
2. An AI by Zapier step assigns a sentiment (positive, neutral, or negative) and one theme from a fixed list you provide, such as price, quality, delivery, or support.
3. The response and tags are saved to a table.
4. Digest by Zapier collects summaries through the week.

**Action:** negative feedback sends you an immediate email; everything else arrives in a weekly digest email.

**Expected benefit:** you spot recurring problems and praise without reading every response the moment it arrives.

**Watch out for:** themes drifting over time. Use a fixed list of themes in your instructions so results stay comparable week to week.

## 8. Build a weekly research digest

**Trigger:** a new row in a Google Sheet where you save links and notes during the week.

**Tools involved:** Google Sheets, AI by Zapier, Digest by Zapier, Gmail.

**Workflow:**

1. You add a link and a short note to your research sheet whenever you find something useful.
2. The Zap triggers on each new row, and an AI by Zapier step writes a two-sentence summary from your note.
3. Digest by Zapier adds each summary to a running weekly digest.

**Action:** once a week, the digest is emailed to you as a single reading list.

**Expected benefit:** research you save during busy days becomes a short weekly review instead of a forgotten list of tabs.

**Watch out for:** summaries built from thin notes. The AI step only knows what you give it, so write a sentence about why each link matters.

## How to build your first AI Zap

1. **Pick one example** that matches a task you do every week.
2. **Create a Zap** and set up the trigger app and event. Use Copilot if you prefer to describe the workflow in plain language.
3. **Test the trigger** with a real recent example, such as an actual email or form entry.
4. **Add the AI step** and write specific instructions: what to read, what to output, the exact format, and what to do when unsure.
5. **Add filters or paths** so the Zap stops or branches when the AI output is missing or unexpected.
6. **Add the action** and make it a draft, label, or notification at first.
7. **Test with several examples,** including unusual ones.
8. **Turn it on and check the Zap's run history** regularly during the first weeks.

## Tips for reliable Zapier AI automations

- **Keep outputs structured.** Ask for short fixed categories or labelled fields rather than free text when later steps depend on them.
- **Send only the data you need.** Avoid passing sensitive information into AI steps unless it is required.
- **Watch your task usage.** Multi-step and high-volume Zaps use your allowance faster.
- **Document your Zaps.** Name each step clearly so you remember what it does months later.
