At some point every growing solo business hits the same wall: there is more admin than hours. The two obvious fixes are software and people. AI tools promise to do routine work for the price of a subscription. A virtual assistant (VA) offers a real person who can handle what software cannot. This guide compares the two honestly, including costs, and gives you a simple way to decide which tasks belong where.

### What a virtual assistant costs

We have not published average VA rates because there is no single number that holds true for most readers. A VA's rate depends on:

- **Location:** rates differ substantially between countries and regions, reflecting local costs of living and labor markets.
- **Experience and specialization:** general admin support usually costs less than bookkeeping, marketing, or executive assistance.
- **Hiring route:** hiring directly, through a freelance marketplace, or through an agency. Marketplaces and agencies may add fees on top of the VA's rate.
- **Commitment:** ad hoc hours, a monthly retainer, or a fixed package.
- **Your time zone needs:** requiring overlap with your working hours can narrow your options.

The reliable way to know what a VA will cost you is to write a short task list and ask for quotes from three to five candidates or agencies.

### A worked example with stated assumptions

The numbers below are **illustrative assumptions, not market rates.** Replace them with quotes you receive and the tool prices that apply to you.

**Assumptions:**

- A VA at an assumed **$15 per hour** for **10 hours per week**.
- An average of **4.33 weeks per month** (52 weeks ÷ 12).
- An AI setup of ChatGPT Plus ($20 per month) plus Zapier Professional on annual billing ($19.99 per month).
- **Your time** spent setting up and reviewing the AI tools: an assumed 2 hours per week.

**Monthly cost:**

- **VA:** $15 × 10 hours × 4.33 weeks ≈ **$650 per month**, before any platform or agency fees.
- **AI tools:** $20 + $19.99 ≈ **$40 per month**, plus about 8.7 hours of your own time (2 hours × 4.33 weeks).

On price alone the software looks far cheaper. But the two options do not do the same work. Those 10 VA hours might include calling suppliers, handling a difficult client email, or sorting a messy inbox, none of which the AI setup can do. The better question is: **for each task, what is the cheapest reliable way to get it done well?**

## A practical decision framework

### Step 1: List your recurring tasks

Write down everything you do at least weekly that is not your core paid work. Be specific: "reply to booking enquiries", not "email".

### Step 2: Score each task on four questions

1. **Is it rules-based?** Could you write clear instructions that cover almost every case?
2. **Is it digital?** Does all the information live in apps that can connect to other tools?
3. **What is the cost of a mistake?** Would an error annoy a customer, lose money, or create legal risk?
4. **Does it need a relationship or a conversation?**

### Step 3: Assign the task

- **Rules-based, digital, low-risk:** automate with AI tools, and check the output at first.
- **Needs judgment or conversation, but routine:** delegate to a VA.
- **High-risk or core to your business:** keep it yourself, using AI for drafts if helpful.
- **Mixed:** delegate to a VA who uses AI tools for the repeatable parts.

## The hybrid approach

You do not have to choose one side. A common pattern for growing solo businesses:

1. **Automate first** the tasks that are clearly rules-based, such as booking, meeting notes, and moving form data.
2. **Hire a VA for a small number of hours** for the remaining tasks that need a person.
3. **Give your VA suitable AI tools** so their paid hours go to judgment work, not typing.
4. **Review monthly.** As processes become clearer, move more steps into automation.

## Risks to manage with either option

- **Data access.** Whether you connect an AI tool or give a VA access, share only what is needed. Use separate accounts and a password manager instead of sharing your own logins.
- **Confidentiality.** Check your client agreements before sharing client information with software or contractors. Consider a written confidentiality agreement with a VA.
- **Silent failures.** Automations can stop working without warning. Check them regularly.
- **Single points of failure.** Document your processes so work continues if a VA leaves or a tool changes.
- **Employment rules.** How you engage a contractor has legal and tax implications that vary by country. Get local advice if you are unsure.
