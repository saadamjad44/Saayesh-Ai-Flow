In six weeks, three of the largest AI companies shipped the same idea: an agent that contacts you first. Meta launched Muse on 8 September 2026, OpenAI released Dots on 29 September, and Anthropic previewed Claude Duet on 21 October.

The coverage of all three answers what they are. It mostly skips the question a reader actually arrives with, which is whether they can have one — because all three are gated, and the gates are different. One is waitlisted, one needs a plan most people do not have, and all three are limited by country.

So this comparison starts with eligibility rather than capability. Features you cannot access are not features.

If you only want to know about the newest of the three, [what is Claude Duet](/ai-agents/what-is-claude-duet/) explains that product on its own — how it works, what it connects to, and where it stops.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **All three are proactive agents** — they work in the background and message you first, rather than waiting for a prompt.
- **Eligibility, not quality, is the real difference today:** Duet is a US-only waitlist, Muse is US and Canada, and Dots excludes Pro users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland.
- **Claude Duet is the one aimed at business owners,** by Anthropic's own positioning, and sits on the cheapest qualifying plan at Pro.
- **OpenAI Dots is the most expensive gate** — realistically the 100-dollar-a-month tier — and the strongest fit if you already live in ChatGPT.
- **Meta Muse is the easiest to try and the least business-shaped:** free tier, consumer tasks, shopping as its top use.
- **No page can rank these honestly yet,** including this one. Two are previews or staged rollouts, and nobody has run all three through real work.

</KeyTakeaways>

That middle column is the comparison. **Duet is the only one of the three whose vendor describes small business owners as the intended user**, and its headline features — a daily rundown, dashboards, forecasts — are the shape of someone running something rather than someone running errands.

Muse points the other way. Meta reported more than 10 million users across the US and Canada by its Q3 2026 earnings, with shopping the top activity and demand high enough that it needed Stripe to raise credit limits. That is a consumer product succeeding as a consumer product. It is also the one you can try today for nothing, which has its own value: an hour with Muse teaches you more about what a proactive agent feels like than any article will.

Dots sits between them and is decided almost entirely by what you already pay for. If ChatGPT is where your work happens and you are on Pro or Business Premium, Dots is an addition to a tool you know. If you are not, it is the most expensive way into this category.

## The part nobody can tell you yet

There is no honest ranking of these three, and you should be suspicious of any page that offers one this month.

Duet is a research preview that most people, including us, cannot open. Dots is still rolling out. Muse has scale but on a different task set. Nobody has run all three through the same month of real work, which means every "best proactive AI agent" list published right now is reading the same launch posts you can read yourself.

What can be said is narrower and more useful:

- **The gates are real and they move.** Two days separated Duet's announcement from this page. Assume the limits in the table above are temporary in both directions.
- **An agent's reach depends on other companies agreeing to be reached.** Amazon has blocked Muse from shopping on its site. That constraint applies to every agent in this category and sits outside your control and the vendor's.
- **Approval boundaries differ, and they matter more than features.** Each vendor draws a different line around what its agent may do alone — Duet's are published in some detail and broken down in [what is Claude Duet](/ai-agents/what-is-claude-duet/). Whichever you use, find the equivalent boundary before you connect anything, and keep a human inside it for everything that spends money or sends mail in your name.

## The question behind the question

Most people searching for these products have a different problem than the one a proactive agent solves.

A proactive agent earns its cost when you have more incoming streams than you can personally watch — inboxes, calendars, pipelines, dashboards — and the useful work is noticing things and surfacing them. That is a real situation, and it is the one Anthropic describes when it says Duet is for people with too much to track.

It is not the common situation. The common situation is a handful of tasks that happen the same way every time: an enquiry arrives and needs logging, an invoice needs chasing, a form submission needs routing somewhere. That work does not need software that decides its own steps. It needs software that follows yours, which is cheaper, more predictable, and does not require a waitlist.

## What to do this week

If you are eligible and curious, **join the Claude Duet waitlist** — it costs nothing beyond a plan you may already have, and being early in a preview is the only way to form a real opinion rather than borrowing one. Read [what is Claude Duet](/ai-agents/what-is-claude-duet/) first if you want to know what you are joining, including the usage limits it shares with the rest of your plan.

If you are on a ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium plan already, **try Dots on a recurring job you would otherwise do manually on a Monday morning**. You are not evaluating a purchase; you already made it.

If you are in the US or Canada and want to understand the category for free, **spend an hour with Muse** on something low-stakes. Treat it as education, not procurement.

And if you are anywhere else, or none of this maps onto a problem you actually have, **do nothing** — which is a real answer and usually the right one with a six-week-old product category. The thing worth doing instead is working out which of your tasks is repetitive enough to automate, because that will still be true when these products open up.
