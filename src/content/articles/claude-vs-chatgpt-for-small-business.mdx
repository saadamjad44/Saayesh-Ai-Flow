Claude and ChatGPT are the two general AI assistants a small business is most likely to be choosing between, and the usual comparison - which one writes better - is the least useful question you can ask about them. By late 2026 both products have moved well past the chat box. Both connect to business software, both produce real spreadsheets and documents, both run tasks on a schedule, and both sell a team plan at the same seat price.

What separates them is the shape of the work they are built around. This comparison goes workflow by workflow through a small-business week and says, for each one, what each vendor currently documents and which tool that points to.

## How this comparison was researched

This is a research-based comparison, not a hands-on review. Saayesh AI Flow has not run the two products side by side in a live business, so there are no scores, no winner totals, no time-saved figures and no accuracy percentages here.

Every product fact below comes from the vendors' own current material, reviewed on 6 October 2026: Anthropic's [Claude pricing page](https://claude.com/pricing), [Claude for Small Business solutions page](https://claude.com/solutions/small-business), [small-business plugin listing](https://claude.com/marketplace/plugins/small-business), [Claude for Microsoft 365 page](https://claude.com/claude-for-excel), [Excel add-in documentation](https://claude.com/docs/office-agents/excel) and [Team plan support article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9266767-what-is-the-team-plan); and OpenAI's [ChatGPT Business overview](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8792828-chatgpt-business-overview), [ChatGPT for small business programme](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-small-business-program/), and its help-centre articles on [apps in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11487775-apps-in-chatgpt), [scheduled tasks](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt), [workspace agents](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001143-chatgpt-workspace-agents-for-enterprise-and-business) and [data controls](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt).

Three kinds of statement are kept apart on purpose. **Product facts** are attributed to the vendor that publishes them. **Editorial assessment** is labelled as ours. **Recommendations** are judgements about workflow fit based on those documented capabilities, not test results. Both products changed materially in the weeks before this was written, so check anything decision-critical against the links above.

## 1. Business research and analysis

For the research a small business actually does - checking a supplier, reading a contract, working out what last quarter meant - both products are capable, and no vendor documentation supports ranking them.

**Claude.** Anthropic's pricing page lists web search, file creation and code execution on the Free plan, with paid plans adding projects, task scheduling, Claude Code and the Microsoft 365 and Chrome extensions. Research that draws on your own material goes through connectors: on the Team plan those cover Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, GitHub, Microsoft 365 and Slack, with enterprise search across them.

**ChatGPT.** OpenAI lists deep research as included on both Standard and Premium Business seats. The more interesting feature for a small business is company knowledge, which OpenAI describes as letting ChatGPT "use your organization's context from your connected apps to give answers specific to your company and projects, with clear citations and links back to the original sources," across connectors including Slack, SharePoint, Google Drive, GitHub, HubSpot and Asana, respecting each user's existing permissions.

**Our assessment.** The citation behaviour is the practical difference, and on documented evidence it favours ChatGPT: an answer that links back to the source file is one you can check, and checking is the part of research AI does not remove. Treat that as our reading of the two vendors' documentation, not a measured result.

## 2. Writing, editing and business documents

Both products draft, rewrite, shorten and restructure text competently. Anyone claiming a clear winner here is reporting a preference, not a fact. What differs is where the finished file lands.

**Claude** works inside Microsoft Office. Anthropic states that "Claude for Excel, PowerPoint, and Word are generally available on all paid Claude plans," with Claude for Outlook in beta on paid plans, and describes every edit as reviewable - tracked changes in Word, highlighted cells in Excel - while preserving "your heading styles, slide masters, formula conventions, and numbering." Since 16 September 2026 Claude Docs and Claude Slides also create documents and decks inside a conversation, in beta on paid plans.

**ChatGPT** works through ChatGPT Work, which OpenAI describes as creating and editing "documents, spreadsheets, presentations, reports, and analyses," with native support for Google Docs, Sheets and Slides when the Google Workspace app is enabled, and Excel support through the ChatGPT for Excel add-in on the desktop app.

**Our assessment.** This section is decided by your file format, not by writing quality. A business that lives in Word and Excel and cares about tracked changes has more documented support from Claude; a business on Google Workspace has more from ChatGPT. For marketing content production at volume, where brand consistency across several people matters more than drafting speed, read our [Jasper vs Claude vs ChatGPT](/ai-writing-tools/jasper-vs-claude-vs-chatgpt/) comparison instead - that is a different product category and a different decision.

## 3. Long documents, PDFs and internal knowledge

Context window - how much text a model can hold at once - is the figure most often quoted in comparisons like this one and the one most often quoted wrongly. Before the numbers, the honest caveat: **neither vendor publishes a single product-level context limit, and the headline figures are not directly comparable.** Treat what follows as a pointer to the page you should check for your own plan, not as a score.

**What Anthropic publishes.** Its [context window article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8606394-how-large-is-the-context-window-on-paid-claude-plans) gives separate figures per product surface. For chatting with Claude, the newest models are listed at 1M tokens, Opus 4.8 and similar at 500K, and most other models at 200K. In Claude Code, 1M requires selecting a specific model variant, and on Pro it requires usage credits to be enabled. In Cowork, Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 are listed at 200K, and Sonnet 5 "automatically compacts the conversation at 500K tokens." Separately, Anthropic's own Team plan page still describes a flat "200k context window". Those pages are not fully consistent with one another, so the only reliable figure is the one shown for the specific model, plan and surface you intend to use.

**What OpenAI publishes.** For ChatGPT Business, a 128K-token context window for the Instant and Thinking models and 272K for the Pro model, with per-seat limits on thinking models, deep research and image generation, and an optional shared credit pool for when a seat runs out. Personal plans are documented separately and differently again - the Plus figure is expressed as a combined total split between input and output rather than as a single window.

**Why we are not scoring this.** A nominal window is not the same as usable room: Anthropic documents automatic summarising of earlier messages as a conversation grows, and OpenAI's Plus figure counts output against the same total. So a larger published number on one side does not establish that one product handles your documents better. What it does tell you is that if your work routinely involves a single very large file, this is a specification worth checking carefully on both vendors' pages before you subscribe, rather than a question either article or vendor can settle for you.

For internal knowledge rather than single long files, the comparison flips. Claude Team includes projects and knowledge bases with enterprise search across connected tools. ChatGPT's company knowledge searches connected apps on demand, and OpenAI's synced connectors for Google Drive and SharePoint "sync and index selected content from your connected sources in advance," which it says improves speed and answer quality without re-querying each source.

**Our assessment.** If your hard problem is years of files scattered across Drive, SharePoint and Slack, ChatGPT's indexed connectors and permission-aware citations are aimed directly at it. If your hard problem is one very large single document, Claude's per-model figures reach higher on paper, but check the limit for the model you would actually use before treating that as decisive. Both statements are about documented design, not tested retrieval quality.

## 4. Customer support and inbound work

Neither product is a help desk, and neither vendor sells one to small businesses. What they sell is drafting and triage on top of systems you already run.

**Claude.** The small-business plugin includes named workflows for inbound work - `/speed-to-lead`, `/grow-pipeline` and `/call-list` - drawing on HubSpot, Salesforce and accounting connectors. Anthropic's solutions page describes lead responses as "automatic replies with scheduling," and is explicit that Claude "drafts and prepares. You decide what goes out."

**ChatGPT.** OpenAI documents event-triggered tasks that "run in Work and respond to supported Gmail, Slack, or GitHub activity," where a Gmail task can respond to a new message or filter by sender or subject. Workspace agents can be built for repeatable work, connected to apps, shared with teammates, used in Slack, and run on a schedule or triggered through an API.

**Our assessment.** For support specifically, ChatGPT's email and Slack triggers map more directly onto how support actually arrives in a small business, which is as messages rather than as records. Claude's advantage appears when the reply needs a number - an order status, a balance, an invoice date - because the connector is already attached to the system holding it. In both cases our recommendation is the same, and it is not a product feature: keep customer-facing replies in review until you have read enough of them to know what the tool gets wrong.

## 5. Marketing workflows

The marketing comparison has one decisive, documented asymmetry.

Anthropic's support documentation states plainly that Claude "doesn't generate photos or illustrations the way image-generation tools do" and is an image-understanding model; it builds diagrams, charts and interactive visuals in HTML and SVG instead. ChatGPT generates and edits images natively in the product.

Beyond images, both sides have something aimed at marketing work. Claude's plugin includes `/marketing-monday` and a Canva connector. OpenAI's small-business programme lists partner plugins and skills from Shopify, Intuit, Dropbox, Slack, Atlassian and Wix, with Shopify's covering store setup, inventory, discount codes, orders and store analytics.

**Our assessment.** If your marketing output includes visuals you would otherwise pay for or make yourself, ChatGPT removes a step Claude cannot. If your marketing is text, scheduling and reporting against store or CRM data, the two are close and your connector list decides it. For the tools built specifically for marketing output rather than general assistants, see our [AI tools for marketing](/ai-by-industry/ai-tools-for-marketing/) guide.

## 6. Spreadsheets, data and technical work

Both vendors now ship spreadsheet integrations, and the differences are specific enough to decide on.

**Claude for Excel** is generally available to Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans. Anthropic documents answers with cell-level citations, adjusting assumptions while keeping formula relationships intact, finding the root cause of `#REF!` and `#DIV/0` errors, building models from a plain-language description, and native operations including sorting, filtering, pivot table edits, conditional formatting and data validation dropdowns. It also publishes the limits: no data tables, no macros or VBA, no support on Excel 2016 or 2019 perpetual licences or on iPad, and a note that it is "not recommended for" final client deliverables without human review or audit-critical calculations without verification. Anthropic additionally warns to "only use Claude for Excel with trusted spreadsheets," because external files can carry hidden instructions.

**ChatGPT for Excel and Google Sheets** is documented as available to Business, Enterprise, Edu and K-12 users and to Free, Go, Pro and Plus users, with limited usage on Free and Go and agentic usage limits on Plus and Pro. OpenAI describes it as suited to building trackers, budgets, plans and templates, cleaning up messy sheets, understanding an unfamiliar spreadsheet, updating a model when inputs change, and running scenarios and sensitivity tables. Separately, ChatGPT analyses uploaded files with Python-based calculations, transformations and statistical analysis, producing tables and charts.

**Our assessment.** Claude is the better-documented choice for working inside an existing Excel model you must not break, largely because of the cell-level citations and formula-preserving edits. ChatGPT is the better-documented choice if you are on Google Sheets, if you want spreadsheet help on a free or $8 plan, or if your real task is analysing a CSV rather than maintaining a model. On coding, both have a dedicated surface - Claude Code and Codex respectively - and for most small businesses that is a developer question rather than an operations one.

## 7. Integrations and connected business workflows

This is the section most likely to settle the decision, and the terminology changed on both sides during 2026, so it is worth using the current names.

**Claude.** Anthropic sells a specific small-business product: a plugin its listing described, when we checked it on 6 October 2026, as 43 ready-to-run workflows working with 37 partner connectors. Both counts have risen twice since the May 2026 launch, so treat them as a snapshot and read the current listing for your own stack. The workflows are named commands rather than freeform prompts - `/monday-brief`, `/close-month`, `/plan-payroll`, `/pay-the-bills`, `/restock`, `/tax-prep`, `/speed-to-lead`, `/grow-pipeline`, `/call-list`, `/marketing-monday`, and `/smb-onboard` for setup. The named connectors include Intuit QuickBooks, Xero, PayPal, Stripe, Square, HubSpot, Salesforce, Shopify, Gusto, Canva, Docusign, Slack, Zapier, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. The solutions page notes that "every workflow also runs from a spreadsheet or a forwarded email," so a business with unconnected records is not locked out entirely.

**ChatGPT.** OpenAI's equivalent layer is apps and plugins. Since 9 July 2026 the app directory has been the Plugin directory, where a plugin listing can bundle apps, skills and app templates; apps remain the integrations that connect ChatGPT and Codex to external data and actions. For a small business the relevant pieces are the connectors behind company knowledge - Slack, SharePoint, Google Drive, GitHub, HubSpot, Asana and more - and the partner plugins and skills in OpenAI's small-business programme from Dropbox, Shopify, Intuit, Slack, Atlassian and Wix.

**Our assessment.** The two connector lists serve different halves of a business. Claude's is weighted towards the systems of record that hold money: accounting, payments, payroll, store and CRM. ChatGPT's is weighted towards the places work gets discussed and filed: chat, drive, documents, tickets. So the test is not which list is longer. It is which of your systems holds the information behind the task you repeat most often, and whether the tool you are considering can read it. Our deeper look at the Claude side is in [Claude for Small Business: 8 practical workflows](/ai-business-tools/claude-for-small-business/).

## 9. Automation and repeatable workflows

Both products will run work without you starting it, and both are more limited than the word "agent" suggests. Neither vendor documents anything resembling a business running itself.

**What Claude documents.** Scheduled tasks are available on all paid plans and run remotely, so they continue when your computer is asleep. Autonomy on the small-business workflows is graduated and reversible: "Every workflow starts in approval mode: Claude drafts and stages the work, and nothing sends, posts, or pays until you say so. Once you trust a workflow, you can let it run on its own schedule." Some steps are built to stop regardless - the solutions page states that "payroll is set up for you to submit, bills wait for your approval before they're paid, and Claude doesn't file anything for you." One naming note, because it affects what you will see on screen: since 16 September 2026 Anthropic has been merging Cowork, its agentic surface, into the main Claude experience. As of 6 October 2026 its pricing page states that "Claude Cowork is now just Claude," rolling out to Pro and Max with more plans to follow, so depending on your plan you may still see either name, and some documentation still refers to Cowork as a separate surface.

**What ChatGPT documents.** Scheduled tasks run one-time or recurring, up to once per hour with exact delivery times on eligible paid plans, and no more than once a day on Free. Event-triggered tasks respond to supported Gmail, Slack and GitHub activity. Workspace agents, documented as generally available in ChatGPT Business as of 6 October 2026, can be built for repeatable work, given a model and reasoning effort, connected to apps and tools, shared with teammates, used in Slack, and run on a schedule or triggered through an API. Team tasks let several people share responsibility for recurring work.

**Our assessment.** These are two different products aimed at the same wish. Claude hands you finished routines for finance and operations and asks whether your systems match them. ChatGPT hands you the components and asks you to assemble the routine. Pre-built wins when your stack matches the list, because there is nothing to design; build-your-own wins when it does not, because you are not waiting for a connector that may never arrive. Either way, our standing recommendation holds: a process should be boring and well understood before it runs unsupervised, and an automation nobody reads is worse than a manual step, because its failures are silent. Four things that matter more than the headline numbers:

1. **At team level the price is the same.** A standard seat is $25 per user per month on monthly billing, or $20 per user per month on annual billing, on both sides, with a two-seat floor on both. Price is therefore not a tiebreaker for a team; capability fit is.
2. **For one person, ChatGPT has a cheaper rung.** Go has no Claude equivalent, and OpenAI documents spreadsheet help and image generation as available on it with limited usage. OpenAI states Go is available in every country where ChatGPT is supported, at $8 per month in the US, with pricing localised in some markets - so check your own currency rather than assuming the $8. Whether $8 or $20 counts as cheap depends entirely on what it replaces.
3. **Annual billing is a commitment, not just a discount.** Claude Pro's $17 is a per-month equivalent, not a monthly charge: it means $200 billed up front for a full year. The same applies to every annual figure in the table above - the per-seat-per-month price is what you divide by, not what you are billed. Anthropic's small-business page points at Pro and Team specifically and notes that both are month to month, which is the safer way to start.
4. **The connected software is billed separately.** Every workflow in section 7 assumes you already pay for the accounting, CRM, store or payroll product it reads from. Neither assistant replaces any of them.

## 11. Privacy and business data

Only what the vendors state, and none of this is legal advice.

**Model training.** OpenAI states that it does not use content from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise or Edu workspaces, including information accessed through apps, to train its models by default. On personal Free, Plus and Pro accounts, data sharing is enabled by default and controlled by the "Improve the model for everyone" toggle under Data Controls. Anthropic's position is structured the same way round: its consumer documentation for Free, Pro and Max says it may use your chats to improve its models if you choose to allow it, while commercial plans under Claude for Work - Team and Enterprise - are not used for training unless an admin opts into the Development Partner Program.

**The practical reading.** On both sides, the business plan is the one where the training question is answered by default, and a personal plan is the one where the setting is yours to check. If you will paste customer or financial information into either product, that difference is a reason to be on a business plan quite apart from the admin features.

**Access scope.** Every connector is a decision about which business data an assistant can read. OpenAI states that company knowledge "respects your existing company permissions," so each user sees only what they are already authorised to view. Anthropic documents per-connector permission modes you control, and an explicit prompt-injection warning for spreadsheets that came from outside your business. Our recommendation for both products is to connect the two or three systems holding the work you actually repeat, and nothing else.

## 12. Where Claude is the better fit

Scenario by scenario, based on what Anthropic currently documents:

- **A trades or services business whose books, payments and payroll sit in QuickBooks or Xero, Stripe or Square, and Gusto.** The pre-built `/close-month`, `/pay-the-bills` and `/plan-payroll` routines exist for exactly that stack, and they stage work rather than submit it.
- **A business that maintains real Excel models** for pricing, cashflow or job costing, where an edit that breaks a formula chain is the risk. Cell-level citations and formula-preserving updates are documented features, with documented exclusions for macros and VBA.
- **Document-heavy professional work:** contracts, reports, long submissions, anything where one file is the unit of work and Word tracked changes are how review happens.
- **An owner who wants routines rather than a build.** If your systems are on the connector list, the workflows are already written.
- **Any business that wants nothing sent, posted or paid without a human.** Approval by default, with promotion one workflow at a time, is the more conservative model of the two as documented.

## 13. Where ChatGPT is the better fit

- **A business on Google Workspace.** Native Docs, Sheets and Slides work, plus a Google Drive synced connector, is the better-documented path; Claude's Office integration is a Microsoft one.
- **Work that includes images.** This one is binary rather than a matter of degree: Claude does not generate photos or illustrations.
- **Two or more people working on the same material.** Shared projects, Spaces, pages and Teams are built for co-editing in a way Claude's unshareable sessions are not.
- **Scattered institutional knowledge.** If the answer could be in Slack, SharePoint, Drive or a ticket, company knowledge with permission-aware citations is aimed squarely at that.
- **Automation triggered by incoming messages.** Gmail and Slack event triggers are documented; a small-business equivalent on the Claude side is not.
- **A tight budget for one person.** Go is the cheapest paid rung in this comparison, at $8 a month in the US and localised elsewhere.
- **Work spanning more types of task.** Across writing, data, images, voice and code in one subscription, ChatGPT's documented surface area is wider.

## 14. When using both makes sense

Usually it does not, and the reason is overlap: two general assistants will do most of the same jobs, so the second $20 to $25 per seat buys the difference rather than a second assistant.

It can make sense in three situations, each resting on a job one tool documents and the other does not:

1. **Claude for connected finance and operations routines, ChatGPT for everything else.** A business with the matching accounting stack and a genuine need for images or Google-native files has two non-overlapping reasons.
2. **One seat for a specialist, rather than a second subscription for everyone.** If one person maintains the Excel models and nobody else touches them, a single Claude Pro seat alongside the team's ChatGPT workspace is cheaper than moving everyone.
3. **A short, deliberate overlap while deciding.** Running both for a month on real work is a legitimate expense. Running both for a year because nobody cancelled is not.

Before paying for two, write down the task the second tool is for. If you cannot name it in one sentence, you are buying reassurance.

## 15. Which should a small business choose?

A decision framework rather than a verdict, because the inputs are yours.

**Choose Claude if** your finance, payroll, store or CRM data sits in systems on its connector list and you repeat the same process against them weekly; or your work is Microsoft Office documents and Excel models you cannot afford to break; or you want pre-built routines with approval by default rather than something to assemble.

**Choose ChatGPT if** you are on Google Workspace; or images are part of your output; or two or more people need to work on the same material; or your institutional knowledge is scattered across chat, drive and documents; or you want automation that fires when an email or Slack message arrives; or you want the widest spread of capability for one subscription, with the cheapest paid starting point of the two.

**Consider both only if** you can name one documented capability in each that the other lacks, and both of those jobs are genuinely in your week.

**Start with neither if** your answer to most of the above is "not sure." Both have free plans. Spend a fortnight putting the same five real tasks through each - a customer email, a document you know well, a messy spreadsheet, a question about your own numbers, and a piece of marketing - and judge on your own work rather than on anyone's comparison, including this one.
