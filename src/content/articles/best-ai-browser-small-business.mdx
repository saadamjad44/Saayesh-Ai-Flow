## The quick answer

If you want a one-line recommendation before the detail: **stick with Chrome and a ChatGPT or Claude subscription** unless you do heavy research daily. The AI browser market is young and unstable — Atlas already died after less than a year.

If you are going to try one anyway, Perplexity Comet is the lowest-risk choice. Free, runs everywhere, does research well.

## The three contenders

The AI browser landscape shifted hard in mid-2026. OpenAI killed Atlas. The Browser Company pivoted from Arc to Dia. Perplexity launched Comet as a full browser, not just a search engine with a sidebar.

Here is what is left standing in November 2026:

**Perplexity Comet** — A Chromium-based browser with Perplexity's AI search engine built in. Free to use. The pitch: every tab is a research assistant.

**Dia** — The Browser Company's follow-up to Arc. AI baked into the address bar. The pitch: your browser understands what you are working on and helps without you asking.

**ChatGPT Desktop + Chrome Extension** — Not a browser. OpenAI's answer after killing Atlas: a desktop app with a built-in browser for deeper work, plus a Chrome extension that controls your existing tabs. The pitch: ChatGPT everywhere, in every app.

## What each one does well

### Perplexity Comet: the researcher

Comet's core strength is search. Every query runs through Perplexity's AI, which means you get cited answers, not just links. For a small business owner who spends time researching prospects, comparing vendors, or checking facts, this is genuine time savings.

The free tier handles basic AI search and browsing. You do not need an account to start. The agent features — where Comet browses autonomously to complete a task — require Pro at **$20/month**. Early reviews say the agent mode is inconsistent. It works for simple tasks (find the cheapest flight, gather pricing from three competitors) but struggles with multi-step workflows.

**Best for:** research-heavy roles — consultants, content creators, anyone who opens 15 tabs to answer one question.

**Biggest weakness:** locked to Perplexity's models. If you prefer Claude or ChatGPT's reasoning, you cannot switch.

### Dia: the organizer

Dia comes from the team that built Arc, and it shows. Tab management is where Dia leads the field. It reads context across your open tabs, summarizes what you are working on, and suggests next steps. If you are the type who has 40 tabs open and loses track of what you were doing, Dia tries to fix that.

The AI sits in the address bar. Ask it a question and it pulls context from your current tabs. It does not act autonomously the way Comet's agent does — Dia supervises and suggests, you click to execute.

**Price:** The core browser is free. Reports on paid tiers are inconsistent — some sources say $20/month for extended AI features, others list different numbers. Check Dia's own pricing page before committing.

**Best for:** productivity-focused Mac users who value tab organization over raw AI power.

**Biggest weakness:** Mac-only. Requires Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) and macOS 14+. No Windows, no mobile. If your team has mixed hardware, Dia is not an option.

### ChatGPT Desktop + Chrome Extension: the everything app

This is not really a browser. It is OpenAI's post-Atlas strategy: bring ChatGPT's AI into whatever browser you already use, plus a desktop app for heavier work.

The desktop app (launched July 2026) includes a built-in browser, Work mode for projects that span files and websites, Scheduled Tasks, and access to GPT-5.6. It runs on Mac and Windows and is available to all plans, including Free.

The Chrome extension (expanded October 2026) can look across your open tabs, take control of tabs to complete tasks, fill forms, send messages, download files, and organize tabs. You can tag ChatGPT with "@Chrome" to run browser tasks from the ChatGPT app itself.

**Best for:** people already paying for ChatGPT who want AI in their existing workflow without switching browsers.

**Biggest weakness:** you are splitting attention between two apps (Chrome + ChatGPT desktop). It is not as seamless as a purpose-built AI browser. The Chrome extension adds another layer of permissions and potential slowdown.

## The hidden costs nobody mentions

### Data privacy

Every AI browser sends your browsing data through its AI models. That is the whole point — and the whole risk.

- **Comet** sends your queries through Perplexity's servers. One security reviewer flagged prompt-injection concerns with the agent mode.
- **Dia** processes some context locally, but AI features require server calls.
- **ChatGPT Desktop** sends data through OpenAI. The Chrome extension needs broad tab-access permissions.

If you handle client data, medical information, or financial records in your browser, think hard before giving any AI browser access to those tabs.

### Subscription stacking

A Comet Pro subscription ($20/mo) does not replace your ChatGPT Plus subscription ($20/mo) or your Claude Pro subscription. You are adding $240/year on top of whatever AI subscriptions you already pay.

Run the numbers with the [small business AI cost calculator](/ai-business-tools/small-business-ai-cost-calculator/) before committing. If you are already paying for two AI subscriptions, adding a third through an AI browser needs to save you measurable time to justify the cost.

### The switching cost

Your Chrome bookmarks, extensions, saved passwords, and muscle memory all live in Chrome. Moving to a new browser is not a one-hour switch — it is weeks of friction. Every extension that does not work, every autofill that does not fire, every keyboard shortcut that is wrong costs you time.

Comet supports Chrome extensions. Dia plans to but has not fully shipped that support. The ChatGPT option avoids this entirely because you stay in Chrome.

## The honest "don't bother" verdict

Here is what no AI browser company will tell you:

**Chrome + a ChatGPT or Claude subscription handles 90% of what these AI browsers do.** Open Claude in one tab. Open your work in another. Copy and paste between them. It is not glamorous, but it works on every platform, keeps your extensions, and costs nothing beyond the AI subscription you are already paying.

AI browsers are worth it when:
- You do 2+ hours of research daily (→ Comet)
- You manage 30+ tabs and lose track of context (→ Dia)
- You need AI to act on web pages, not just talk about them (→ ChatGPT extension)

AI browsers are NOT worth it when:
- You check email, write documents, and browse social media. That is not an AI browser problem.
- You are already overwhelmed by subscriptions. Adding another $20/month tool when the [AI tool pricing tracker](/ai-business-tools/ai-tool-pricing-tracker/) shows you are overspending is the wrong move.
- Your team uses mixed operating systems. Dia is Mac-only. The ChatGPT extension needs Chrome. Only Comet works everywhere.

## What happened to ChatGPT Atlas?

OpenAI announced in July 2026 that Atlas would shut down. It stopped working on **August 9, 2026**. Atlas only ever shipped on Mac, and the promised Windows, iOS, and Android versions never arrived.

OpenAI says it folded Atlas's capabilities into:
1. The **ChatGPT desktop app** — now with a built-in browser and Work mode
2. The **ChatGPT Chrome extension** — which gained tab control in October 2026
3. **Codex** — merged into the desktop app for coding workflows

If you were an Atlas user, the desktop app is the closest replacement. It does most of what Atlas did, minus the standalone browser chrome.

## MCP and the future of AI browsers

[MCP (Model Context Protocol)](/getting-started/what-is-mcp-model-context-protocol/) is the open standard that lets AI models connect directly to your tools. As of November 2026, ChatGPT supports MCP through its Connectors feature. Neither Comet nor Dia has confirmed MCP support.

This matters because MCP could make the browser less important. If your AI model can talk directly to Stripe, Notion, and your file system through MCP servers, you do not need an AI browser to bridge the gap. The browser becomes just a browser again, and the AI lives in a separate client like Claude Desktop.

Worth watching. Not worth betting on yet.
