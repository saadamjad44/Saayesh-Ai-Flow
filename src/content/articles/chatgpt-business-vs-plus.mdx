**The rule is short: if anyone other than you does company work in ChatGPT, or if customer and client records go into it, you want Business. If you are on your own and only paste things you would be happy to publish, Plus is enough.**

Almost everything else written about this decision turns into a feature list. The features are not what decides it, because the assistant is broadly the same on both tiers. What differs is who owns the account, who can administer it, and what happens by default — and those three are what a small business actually gets wrong.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Plus is a personal subscription. Business is a company workspace** sold per seat, with an admin and company-owned accounts.
- **Business is the tier OpenAI used to call Team.** Same idea, renamed — which is a hint about how durable any price you read is.

- **Pro is a volume tier, not a business tier.** It fixes usage limits and nothing about ownership or administration.
- **Mixing plans across a team is the worst of both** and makes "where does our client data go" unanswerable.

## The training default, and why it is not the real argument

The headline difference everybody quotes is that Business does not train on your workspace content by default, while personal plans do unless you say otherwise.

That is true, and it matters less than it sounds, because the setting exists on personal plans too. One careful person on Plus who has switched training off is in much the same position as the same person on Business.

The difference appears the moment there are two of you. You cannot see whether a colleague's personal account has training on. You cannot set it for them. You cannot check it after the fact, and you certainly cannot promise a client anything about it. The value of a workspace is not that the setting is better — it is that the setting is **yours, once, for everyone**, and you can answer for it.

That is also the honest framing for anyone weighing this against a client's security questionnaire. "We have a company workspace with training disabled at the workspace level" is a statement you can stand behind. "Everyone on my team has been asked to turn it off" is not.

## The argument nobody makes: whose account the work lives in

Here is the part that has nothing to do with AI and decides the question more often than privacy does.

If your staff use ChatGPT on personal Plus accounts, those accounts are theirs. The chat history is theirs. The projects, the instructions they refined over months, the reference files they uploaded — theirs. When they leave, all of it leaves, and there is no administrative route to any of it, because you were never a party to the account.

Six months of accumulated working knowledge about how your business writes, quotes and answers customers walks out of the door with somebody's personal login. People think about this carefully for email and documents, and then hand the same problem to ChatGPT without noticing.

A workspace fixes it in the dullest possible way: the company owns the accounts, an admin removes the person, the work stays. If you need one reason to move a two-person team off personal subscriptions, it is this one rather than anything on the security page.

## When Plus is the right answer

Stay on Plus when all of the following are true:

- **You are the only person doing company work in it.** Not "mostly me" — only you.
- **You can describe what you paste into it as low-sensitivity.** Drafts, public information, your own thinking, things you would be relaxed about seeing elsewhere.
- **No client has asked you anything in writing** about how you handle their data in AI tools.
- **You have actually opened settings and turned training off.** If you have not done this, do it before reading on; it is the cheapest improvement available to you today.

The seat minimum is what makes this clear-cut. A solo owner on Business is buying seats nobody sits in, for administration features that administer one person. That is not a privacy upgrade, it is a rounding error in your favour pointing the other way.

## When to move to Business

Move when any single one of these is true — they do not need to stack:

- **A second person does company work in ChatGPT.** This is the usual trigger and the clearest one.
- **Customer, client, financial or staff records go in.** Anything you would not want in a training set or in a departing employee's account.
- **A client, insurer or tender has asked** how you handle data in AI tools and expects a verifiable answer.
- **You need the work to survive the person.** Shared projects, company-owned accounts, one bill.
- **You have lost track of who is paying for what.** Several personal subscriptions reimbursed through expenses is a workspace with none of the benefits.

## What Business does not fix

Worth saying plainly, because the upgrade gets sold as a compliance solution and it is not one.

A workspace does not stop someone pasting something they should not have pasted. It changes the default and gives you a place to set a rule; it does not enforce judgement. The written rule is still your job — which of your records may go into an AI tool, which may not, and who decides the grey ones.

It does not make the output correct either. Everything on this site about checking AI work before it leaves your business applies identically on every tier.
