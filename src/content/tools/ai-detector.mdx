## What the five signals measure

The score is the sum of five transparent checks. Each one measures something real about the text and contributes a capped number of points.

- **Sentence-length variety (25 pts).** Human writing bounces between short and long sentences. Default LLM output is more uniform. We compute the coefficient of variation across sentence lengths — a lower value means more AI-like rhythm.
- **LLM-overused phrases (30 pts).** A dictionary of ~40 phrases that appear far more often in LLM output than in human writing: _delve into_, _navigate the complexities_, _it's important to note_, _ever-evolving landscape_, and so on. The tool counts how many appear and shows them.
- **Transitional-word density (20 pts).** Sentences that open with _However,_, _Additionally,_, _Furthermore,_, _Moreover,_ — in that order, one after another — are a common LLM habit. We count the ratio of transition-opened sentences.
- **Vocabulary variety (15 pts).** The type-token ratio: unique words divided by total words. LLM prose often sits in a middle band; both repetitive and highly varied text score lower on the AI-pattern axis.
- **Contractions and informal voice (10 pts).** Default LLM output avoids contractions (_don't_, _can't_, _it's_). Casual human writing uses them liberally. Fewer contractions per 100 words is a mild formality signal.

Every score is shown with the specific matches that produced it, so you can agree or disagree with any individual signal.

## Why AI detection does not work the way people want

The short, honest answer: there is no classifier — commercial or academic — that reliably distinguishes AI-written text from human-written text. [OpenAI pulled its own AI classifier in 2023](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/) because it was wrong too often. Academic studies have repeatedly shown that:

1. **False positives are common**, especially for non-native English speakers whose writing style can resemble some LLM patterns. In one 2023 study published in _Patterns_, GPT detectors misclassified 61% of TOEFL essays as AI-written.
2. **Countermeasures are trivial.** A short paraphrase pass, light manual editing, or a "humanizer" tool defeats most detectors in seconds.
3. **The underlying task is adversarial.** As models improve, their output moves further from the patterns detectors were trained on. Detection accuracy on a one-year-old model tells you nothing about current output.

Anyone selling AI detection as a high-confidence verdict is overselling. We built this tool to make the patterns visible, not to hand you a judgement.

## When this tool is useful

- **Self-review.** You wrote something, you suspect it sounds generic, and you want to know what pattern-match tools will flag so you can change it before publishing.
- **Spot-checking your own LLM output.** You asked an AI to draft something, and you want to see which markers survived your edit.
- **Understanding the limits.** Paste a passage you wrote yourself and see how it scores. If your own writing comes back as "AI patterns", that tells you something useful about every tool like this one.

## When it is not useful

- **Judging someone else.** A score on their text is not evidence of anything.
- **Short paragraphs.** Under 80 words the signals are too noisy.
- **Translated text, poetry, lists, code, or technical reference material.** These do not match either the "human" or "AI" pattern profiles cleanly.
- **Deciding on grades, pay, or promotions.** If you are using AI detection to make a decision about a person, the right answer is to talk to them instead.
